# G1 findings: fungal UPO diversity across BFD

**Measured 2026-11-08; clade calls re-made by phylogenetic placement 2026-11-09.** Clade numbers
come from `G1_BFD_SUMMARY.md` (generated by `upo_place/bin/summarize_place.py`) and
`data/bfd_place/CLADE_STATS.md` (`upo_place/bin/clade_stats.py`). Per-protein table:
`data/bfd_full/upo_place_bfd_all.tsv.zst` on the shared project (every `upo_sites` column plus the
`place_*` calls). The nearest-neighbour version of this document is in git history; where a finding
changed, the old reading is stated and why it no longer holds.

**How clades are called now.** Each protein is placed (epa-ng) on a 187-leaf reference tree: the 19
characterized enzymes plus 168 curated HTPs from Zamocky et al. 2022, rebuilt in-house
(`data/refset_zamocky2022/`). A clade is called when placements inside it carry like-weight ratio
>= 0.5. Leave-one-out over all 187 leaves: **184/187** for Clades I-V, including 102/104 at 30-52.7 %
and 32/32 below 30 % identity to the nearest characterized enzyme, the range where BFD sits. The
old caller was validated only at >= 52.7 % (13 % of BFD). See `upo_place/README.md`.

## The census

**97,520 PF01328 proteins in 15,137 genomes.** BFD holds 23,451 genomes with gene models, so
**8,314 fungal genomes carry no PF01328 at all.** Maximum copy number in one genome is **144**
(*Sphaerobolus stellatus*, Agaricomycetes), followed by seven *Mycena* species at 67-106.

| placement clade | n | catalytic core | Glu180 | mean length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clade I (= Zamocky Family II, long) | 41,679 | 5,081 | 13.9 % | 427 |
| Clade II | 3,547 | 2,835 | 94.2 % | 301 |
| Clade III | 431 | 320 | 82.1 % | 286 |
| Clade IV | 8,437 | 7,407 | 96.2 % | 264 |
| Clade V | 2,425 | 1,535 | 74.1 % | 254 |
| outside Clades I-V | 41,001 | 17,383 | 55.1 % | 275 |

57.1 % of proteins are assigned at LWR >= 0.95; 2.5 % carry `long_pendant` (farther from every
reference than any correct leave-one-out call; kept and flagged).

## Finding 1: about a third of fungal PF01328 has the full catalytic set, and Glu180 is lineage-structured

PF01328 is a broad heme-thiolate peroxidase family, not a UPO-specific one. Using the catalytic
residues that the 2025 panel shows in all 19 characterized UPOs (Cys39, Glu109, Asp111, Glu180),
**34,561 of 97,520 proteins (35.4 %) carry the full set.** This count does not depend on the clade
caller.

Among proteins close to a characterized enzyme (old identity >= 52.7 %, n = 12,818), Glu180 is
present in **93.7 %**, which reproduces the published "universal Glu180" claim independently.

**But its absence is not a per-protein test for "not a UPO".** Placement shows Glu180 is missing in
whole lineages:

| group | Glu180 |
|---|---|
| Clade IV | 96.2 % |
| Clade II | 94.2 % |
| Clade I, basidiomycete members | 66.9 % |
| Clade I, ascomycete members | **1.5 %** (Ala in most) |
| Basidiomycetous 1st (Zamocky Fig 1, outside I-V) | **0.2 %** (11/6,378; curated members carry Asn/Ser) |

**Consequence:** the catalytic-core set (34,561) is a *strict* UPO definition, and it is the right
set wherever strict is needed. By construction it excludes nearly all ascomycete long UPOs and the
Basidiomycetous 1st lineage. Those must be reported as excluded lineages, not as non-UPOs, until
the open question in Finding 2 is answered. The earlier version of this finding read low Glu180
outside the validated range as "informative about those proteins"; it is informative about their
lineage.

## Finding 2: Clade I is Zamocky's Family II, and its ascomycete members lack Glu180

*Overturns the 2026-11-08 Finding 2 ("the Clade I bin at low identity is not trustworthy").*

The earlier reading: Clade I calls carry Glu180 at 16.8 %, all five characterized Clade I enzymes
have it, so the low-identity Clade I bin must be absorbing PF01328 proteins that are not UPOs.

Placement does not support that. **99.8 % of old Clade I calls (33,364/33,424) place inside Family
II**, the long-UPO clade of the reference tree (31 leaves, UFBoot 97-100; leave-one-out 31/31). With
the curated references, Clade I grows to **41,679** (mean length 427 aa, consistent with long UPOs),
and the Glu180 deficit has a clear structure:

| Clade I | n | Glu180 | residue at HspUPO 180 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ascomycota | 33,783 | 1.5 % | Ala 28,408, Ser 2,244, Thr 1,124 |
| Basidiomycota | 7,895 | 66.9 % | Glu 5,280 |

All 13 curated ascomycete Family II sequences in Zamocky 2022 also carry A/S/T at this position; the
five characterized Clade I enzymes are basidiomycete and carry Glu. So the deficit is a property of
**ascomycete long UPOs**, not contamination. Largest lineages: Dothideomycetes 18,744,
Sordariomycetes 9,083, Agaricomycetes 6,292.

**Open, and the next thing to measure:** whether ascomycete long UPOs lack the acid-base Glu, or
carry it at a position that does not align to HspUPO 180. A structure-based alignment (or a predicted
structure) of one ascomycete long UPO against AaeUPO settles it. Until then neither "they are
inactive" nor "they are normal UPOs" is supported.

## Finding 3 (the headline): the clade with the most intact UPO machinery rests on one characterized enzyme

**Clade IV is now a supported clade with 20 references, not a one-reference bin**, and it is still
the clearest target for characterization.

- **In the tree:** MphUPO plus 19 curated ascomycete HTPs (Botrytis, Sclerotinia, Aspergillus,
  Fusarium, Colletotrichum, ...), UFBoot 96-97. Leave-one-out 20/20.
- **In BFD:** **8,437 proteins**, the most catalytically intact clade: **96.2 % Glu180**, **7,407**
  in the catalytic core (7,395 at LWR >= 0.95). Median identity to the nearest characterized enzyme
  62.8 %.
- **Lineages:** Sordariomycetes 3,441, Dothideomycetes 2,954, Eurotiomycetes 1,690, Leotiomycetes
  340; essentially no basidiomycetes (7).
- It is larger than the old call (6,922): **2,853 proteins the old caller put in Clade V** (nearest
  reference HspUPO or TteUPO) place in Clade IV.

**And its only characterized member is still MphUPO**: *Macrophomina phaseolina*, listed in the 2025
study's Table S1 as "Putative UPO", T1 (4-propylguaiacol peroxygenation) 0-5 % from n = 1. The 19
added references are curated genome predictions, not characterized enzymes.

So the best-delimited, most catalytically intact, second-largest assigned UPO clade in fungi has no
measured activity beyond one weak measurement on one enzyme. **Recommendation, unchanged and now
better supported: characterize a Clade IV enzyme.**

### Caveats on Finding 3

- The clade's *boundary* is now tested (tree support, leave-one-out); its *activity* is not. MphUPO's
  low T1 does not license "Clade IV is inactive".
- The 2,853 proteins moved from old Clade V depend on the placement boundary between IV and V, which
  sits on a pectinate run of short branches in the reference tree. They are assigned at high LWR, but
  this is the boundary most likely to move with a denser reference set.
- **Rank versus Clade V is supported but marginal.** On the 19-reference tree (skylark,
  `PLACEMENT_FINDINGS.md`) MphUPO fell inside Clade V on nodes with 20-28 % bootstrap. On the
  187-leaf tree Clade IV is recovered at SH-aLRT/UFBoot 83/96 and Clade V at 94/86, IV+V at
  98.5/99. Say "Clade IV, sister to Clade V", not "a distinct subtype". `RECONCILIATION.md` §2.
- Clade calls at low identity to the nearest surviving reference are under test
  (`RECONCILIATION.md` §1); the counts in this document follow its decision rule.

| Assigned, LWR 0.5-0.95 | 796 | 0.8 % |

| Outside Clades I-V | 39,770 | 40.8 % |
| **Catalytic core and assigned to Clades I-V** | **17,178** | **17.6 %** |

The old statement was "roughly 8 % of proteins have a clade call that is both inside the validated
identity range and anchored on a clade with more than one reference." That measured the reach of a
19-enzyme nearest-neighbour caller. With placement validated across the identity range, the working
set for downstream analysis is the **17,178** catalytic-core proteins with a Clade I-V call, and the
**17,383** catalytic-core proteins outside Clades I-V are a separate population (Finding 4a), not an
error bin.

Validated-range calls of the old caller are confirmed by placement for Clades I, II and IV (100 %)
and V (2,087/2,637). Its extrapolated calls mostly are not: Clade V 319/22,210, Clade III 40/9,075.

### Finding 4a: 41 % of fungal PF01328 lies in lineages with no characterized enzyme

The old caller forced most of these into Clades III and V. Placement puts them in Zamocky's
Figure 1 lineages:

| where (outside Clades I-V) | n | catalytic core |
|---|---|---|
| Family I (short), outside our clades | 6,291 | 3,656 |
| Ascomycetous 1st | 6,228 | 3,636 |
| Basidiomycetous 1st | 6,378 | 8 |
| Basidiomycetous 2nd | 4,394 | 2,421 |
| Glomeromycete | 784 | 122 |
| Mucorales basal | 315 | 308 |
| no supported Figure 1 clade | 16,601 | 7,226 |

These are UPO-like proteins (many with the full catalytic set) in lineages that have **no
characterized enzyme at all**. That is a second, larger axis of "measurable novelty in UPO subtype
lineages" than Clade IV, and it was invisible to the 19-enzyme caller.

## What this changes for the *Pichia* expression plan

Clade IV contains **1,690 Eurotiomycetes proteins, 1,473 with the catalytic core**. If a Eurotiales
source is still wanted (decision D1), Clade IV Eurotiomycetes members are concrete candidates, and
expressing one would characterize the clade that most needs it. A second candidate class now
exists: a catalytic-core member of Ascomycetous 1st or Basidiomycetous 2nd, lineages with no
characterized enzyme.

This is candidate selection by **annotation confidence and catalytic integrity**, not by predicted
activity. No activity score exists; see `RESULT_T2_negative.md`.

## Finding 5: novelty against public protein space, and why the number is mostly about deposition

**Measured 2026-11-08.** DIAMOND blastp of the 7,055 catalytic-core representatives (90 %
dereplication) against NCBI `nr` (523 GB, Jan 2026 build). Job 31865316, 42 minutes. Raw hits:
`data/bfd_full/novelty/reps090_vs_nr.tsv.zst`. This finding does not depend on the clade caller
except for 5a.

**Every one of the 7,055 representatives has a hit in `nr`.** None is unprecedented.

| Best-hit identity | Representatives |
|---|---|
| = 100 % | **2,026** |
| >= 95 % | 2,781 |
| >= 90 % | 3,249 |
| >= 70 % | 5,788 |
| < 70 % ("novel" by this cut) | **1,267 (18.0 %)** |
| < 50 % | 160 |

Median best-hit identity is **87.3 %**.

### The confound is visible in the data

**For 4,413 of 7,055 representatives (62.6 %), the best `nr` hit is to the same genus as the query
genome**, most plausibly the BFD assembly itself or a close relative, already deposited. So this
measures **deposition status at least as much as biological novelty**:

| Lineage | novel (< 70 %) | n |
|---|---|---|
| Tremellomycetes | **54.0 %** | 87 |
| Lecanoromycetes (lichens) | **33.7 %** | 264 |
| Leotiomycetes | 27.6 % | 293 |
| Sordariomycetes | 16.8 % | 1,373 |
| Agaricomycetes | 16.6 % | 3,135 |
| Dothideomycetes | 15.1 % | 1,021 |
| **Eurotiomycetes** | **10.8 %** | 343 |
| Chytridiomycota | 6.2 % | 96 |

The ordering tracks how heavily each group has been sequenced and deposited (*Aspergillus* and
*Penicillium* among the most, lichens among the least), not biological unusualness. Finding 5b quantifies this directly.

### Finding 5b: the confound is now quantified, and it explains the result

*(From the skylark session, merged 2026-11-11; independent of clade calls.)*

**Measured 2026-11-08**, closing the item `PLAN.md` left open as "a nucleotide/WGS comparison is
still required".

Every one of the 23,680 BFD genomes carries a public NCBI assembly accession. The meaningful split
is therefore not public-versus-private but **GCF (RefSeq, where NCBI has annotated the genome and
its proteins are in `nr`) versus GCA-only (assembly public, proteins possibly never deposited)**.

| Assembly source | representatives | novel (< 70 %) |
|---|---|---|
| **GCF** (RefSeq-annotated) | 300 | **2.0 %** |
| **GCA-only** | 6,755 | **18.7 %** |

**A 16.7-point difference, z = 7.4.**

**This is close to a complete explanation of the apparent novelty.** When the source genome's
proteins have been deposited, essentially nothing looks novel (2 %). When they have not, 18.7 %
does. The 18 % headline figure is a statement about annotation deposition, not about fungal
biology.

It also disposes of the lineage ordering in the table above: Eurotiomycetes looked least novel and
lichens most, and that is what deposition rate predicts.

**What this does and does not replace.** It answers the question the nucleotide comparison was
meant to answer — separating novelty from deposition — and answers it more directly, because the
genomes themselves are all public, so a tblastn against WGS would by construction find nearly every
sequence and return a near-zero novelty rate. A literal tblastn-versus-`nt`/WGS run has **not**
been done. Nothing in this project should quote a biological novelty rate for fungal UPOs; the
defensible statement is that **no dereplicated fungal UPO in BFD lacks a public relative, and
apparent novelty tracks whether the source genome has deposited protein annotation.**

The residual 2 % in GCF genomes is most plausibly annotation disagreement — funannotate calling a
gene model NCBI did not — rather than novelty.

### Finding 5a: novelty barely tracks clade

By placement clade (same representatives; best hit = first DIAMOND row, which gives 1,266 novel,
one fewer than above through a tie):

| placement clade | novel (< 70 %) | n |
|---|---|---|
| outside Clades I-V | 20.6 % | 3,251 |
| Clade II | 19.0 % | 594 |
| Clade I | 16.9 % | 1,846 |
| Clade V | 13.7 % | 444 |
| Clade IV | 13.0 % | 775 |
| Clade III | 6.9 % | 145 |

Among the large clades the range is 13-19 %: the subtype a UPO belongs to barely predicts whether it
has a close public relative. Proteins outside Clades I-V are modestly more novel (20.6 %;
Ascomycetous 1st 24.1 %, Basidiomycetous 2nd 20.8 %). Clade IV remains among the least novel:
**under-characterized, not under-sequenced.** The sequences are public; what is missing is
biochemistry.

## Finding 6: lichen and Glomeromycota contamination — checked, no evidence found

*(From the skylark session, merged 2026-11-11; independent of clade calls. The 124 Glomeromycota
UPO genes here are the ones placement puts in the glomeromycete clade, 121 of 124.)*

**Measured 2026-11-08**, closing the `PLAN.md` item "contig-level checks before any lineage claim
is reported". 2,444 UPO genes across 547 genomes in the lineages where symbiont or host
contamination is the standing worry.

The classic contamination signature is a gene sitting alone on a short, gene-poor contig. Tested by
comparing, within each genome, how many protein-coding genes share a UPO's contig against how many
share a **randomly chosen gene's** contig (a gene-weighted baseline, so fragmented assemblies are
compared against their own fragmentation, not against a global expectation).

| Lineage | UPO genes | genes on UPO contig | genes on random-gene contig | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lecanoromycetes (lichens) | 2,176 | 11.0 | 9.0 | **1.22** |
| Arthoniomycetes (lichens) | 132 | 21.0 | 15.0 | **1.40** |
| Glomeromycota | 124 | 13.5 | 5.5 | **2.45** |
| Coniocybomycetes | 5 | 36.0 | 25.0 | 1.44 |
| Lichinomycetes | 2 | 47.0 | 33.0 | 1.42 |
| Candelariomycetes | 5 | 40.0 | 107.0 | 0.37 |

**In every lineage with a meaningful sample, UPO genes sit on contigs that are *more* gene-rich
than the typical contig in the same genome**, not less. Glomeromycota UPOs are on contigs carrying
2.45 times as many genes as a random Glomeromycota gene's contig. There is no sign of UPOs being
stranded on short foreign contigs.

Candelariomycetes is below 1, but n = 5 and that is a single genome's worth of genes; no weight
should be put on it.

**What this test does and does not cover.** It detects the common signature — a gene isolated on a
short contig. It does **not** detect contamination carried on a large, well-assembled contig from
another organism, which would need taxonomic assignment of the neighbouring genes. So the honest
statement is: **the standard contig-level contamination signature is absent, and the lineage
counts in Findings 3 and 5 can be reported, with taxonomic verification of neighbours still
available as a stronger check if a specific lineage claim becomes load-bearing.**
