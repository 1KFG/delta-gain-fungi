Candidate effectors of two Mucor strains isolated from Marchantia

Status: in progress. Last updated 2026-11-12. Code: analysis/mucor_secretome/. All analysis ran on the UCR HPCC.

The question

Two Mucor strains, JEM1 and JEM2, were isolated from Marchantia (MorenoLab, shared/projects/MorenoLab/Mucor_genomes). The practical questions are:

  1. Which of their secreted proteins are candidate effectors?
  2. Could any of them interact with host proteins?

Species identity was a secondary question that turned out to matter, because it determines which genomes count as “relatives” when measuring how lineage-restricted a candidate is.

1. Species identity

samples.csv listed JEM1 as M. hiemalis and JEM2 as M. circinelloides. An earlier fastANI run appeared to contradict this: JEM2 matched M. circinelloides at 99.8 %, but JEM1 had no relative above 80 %, its best hit being BFD’s M. hiemalis at 79.5 %.

ITS against UNITE v10 resolved it, with both BFD assemblies included as controls — which is what made the result interpretable.

Assembly Top ITS hit Identity Verdict
JEM1 (cleaned) Mucor hiemalis 99.3 % M. hiemalis
JEM2 (cleaned) Mucor circinelloides 98.6 % M. circinelloides
BFD M. circinelloides (control) Mucor circinelloides 98.8 % control passes
BFD M. hiemalis (control) “Mucorales sp.” 98.5 % M. hiemalis not in its top 5

The mislabelled genome is BFD’s, not MorenoLab’s. JEM1’s low ANI reflects a bad reference, not an unusual query. No correction to samples.csv is needed; the public label on GCA_977110905.1_gzMucHiem1 is the one worth questioning.

Two bugs had to be fixed before this ran at all:

  • UNITE’s QIIME release keeps lineages in sh_taxonomy_*.txt, not in the FASTA headers, so grepping the FASTA for “Mucoromycota” matched nothing. bin/unite_tax.py joins the two.
  • ITSx found no ITS in any of the four assemblies, controls included: the rDNA array is collapsed or broken in all of them. The MorenoLab ITSx result that an earlier version of this analysis would have fallen back on is not ITS either — JEM1’s “full” sequence is SSU, reported with a negative ITS length. The method was changed to blast every Mucoromycota UNITE reference against each assembly, which needs no intact ITS.

2. Contamination

The ITS run found a 100 %-identity Fusarium graminearum ITS in JEM2’s spades assembly. FCS-GX had already flagged 2,233 of its 13,555 contigs as Fusarium venenatum and marked them EXCLUDE; the cleaned assembly has 2,584 contigs. Since the proteome feeding effector calling is predicted from the cleaned assembly, a surviving contaminant would be scored as a Mucor effector. Verified directly rather than assumed:

Assembly Ascomycota ITS hits ≥ 97 % over ≥ 300 bp Contigs hit
JEM1 clean / masked 0 0
JEM2 clean / masked 0 0
JEM2 spades (positive control) 5 1

The positive control firing while the cleaned assemblies stay at zero is what makes this a result rather than an absence of evidence.

3. Annotation

The two strains had only funannotate predict output — no functional annotation — so the BFD predictor set was run over them and over both Marchantia proteomes, matching BFD’s own invocations (Fungi_BFD/nextflow/modules/BFD/*) so that a candidate here means what it means across the 292 BFD Mucoromycota genomes.

Three gaps closed in the process, none cosmetic:

  • PredGPI and TargetP were not being run at all, so two of the four secretion categories (gpi_anchored, mitochondrial) were unreachable for these genomes.
  • CAZymes came from a plain hmmsearch against dbCAN.hmm, which yields family names and no substrates. Tier T2 is defined on dbCAN substrate strings, so it would have been systematically empty.
  • SignalP’s table is built from the GFF3, as merge_signalp.py does, not from prediction_results.txt.

The fold set is deliberately not T3. A protein enters it if it is secreted or GPI-anchored and either carries no Pfam domain and is ≤ 300 aa (unknown_small), or is tier T1 (chitin_active). T3’s cysteine thresholds are a reasonable prior for classical effectors but would discard exactly the cysteine-poor unknowns a structure is most informative about: filtering on a sequence feature is self-defeating when the premise is that sequence tells us nothing.

5. Lineage restriction

Candidates were clustered with the 195,284 secreted candidates of all 292 BFD Mucoromycota genomes (MMseqs2, 50 % identity, 80 % coverage) and each family’s reach measured.

Reach JEM1 JEM2 JEM2, conspecifics hidden
genome_specific 105 4 13
species_restricted 3 5 0
genus_restricted 22 19 18
mucorales_wide 103 173 170
mucoromycota_wide 53 47 47

105 against 4 looks like a large biological difference, and the first thing to ask is whether it is an artefact of which genomes happen to have been sequenced: JEM2 has 24 conspecific M. circinelloides genomes in BFD, JEM1 has none, because BFD’s single “M. hiemalis” is the mislabelled one. Hiding JEM2’s conspecifics (third column) raises its genome_specific count only from 4 to 13 — so sampling explains part of the gap but not most of it.

Caveat that remains. JEM1 is the only M. hiemalis genome available, so “strain-specific” and “species-specific” cannot be distinguished for it. The honest statement is that JEM1 has ~105 secreted proteins with no homologue in any sequenced Mucoromycota genome, and that this is ~8× JEM2’s matched figure.

7. Host target panel

Built identically for both liverworts, because the isolation host is not recorded anywhere in the MorenoLab project. The two differ in the respect that matters: M. paleacea forms arbuscular mycorrhizal symbiosis and M. polymorpha has lost it.

Receptor kinases enter as their ectodomain, not the full receptor: the interaction surface is outside the membrane, and a cytoplasmic kinase domain would dominate any structure prediction.

Both species carry a comparable LysM receptor complement, so the unrecorded host species does not block the chitin screen. The other differences should not be read biologically yet — M. polymorpha has 20,412 annotated proteins against M. paleacea’s 18,566, and the annotations come from different groups, so counts need normalising first. The panel also makes no orthology claim between the two species; that is a phylogenetic question, not a Pfam one.

8. Why the chitin axis

Mucorales cell walls are chitin and chitosan. In M. paleacea, the LysM pair MpaLYR/MpaCERK1 discriminates symbiosis from immunity by chitin-oligomer length — CO7 triggers immunity, CO4 activates symbiosis genes (Cell 2025). In M. polymorpha, chitin and peptidoglycan are the patterns that trigger a ROS burst while other MAMPs do not (Curr Biol 2023).

That makes a mechanistic screen rather than a fishing expedition:

Fungal side Predicted action Host side
CE4 chitin deacetylase chitin → chitosan, poorly bound by LysM MpLYK1 / MpLYR ectodomains
LysM / CBM50 protein sequesters CO7 before the receptor sees it same, competitively
cerato-platanin binds chitin oligomers same
GH18 / GH20 trims CO7 → CO4: immunity → symbiosis signal same

For LysM biology the decisive interaction is protein–ligand, not protein–protein, so the screen models effector + (GlcNAc)ₙ and receptor ectodomain + (GlcNAc)ₙ at n = 4 and 7 and compares binding, rather than relying on protein–protein iPTM alone.

9. In progress

  • AlphaFold 3, chitin panel — the folds above against both species’ LysM ectodomains and chitinases, plus CO4/CO7 ligands. MSAs computed once per chain and reused across pairs.
  • AlphaFold 3, unknowns — the ~300 candidates ESMFold left below pLDDT 70. Most are mucorales_wide, so homologues exist and an MSA-based predictor has something to work with.

Controls are built in. iPTM has no universal threshold, and the AlphaFold-Multimer figures (> 0.8 confident, < 0.6 likely wrong) were calibrated on large complexes, not small cysteine-rich effectors, where false negatives are common. The run includes 4EBZ (AtCERK1 LysM ectodomain with chito-oligosaccharide) and 6Q76 (AVR-PikD with the Pikp-1 HMA domain) as positives and a cross-pairing as negative. bin/af3_report.py prints the controls first and applies no threshold of its own: a cut is only worth using if the positives clear it and the negatives do not.

10. Limitations

  1. No expression data. Every candidate is a prediction from sequence and structure; none is known to be expressed in planta.
  2. Structure prediction is prioritisation, not evidence of interaction. A confident fold says what a protein resembles. A confident iPTM says two chains can be modelled together. Neither is a binding assay.
  3. JEM1’s lineage restriction is confounded with species sampling (§5).
  4. The Marchantia panel counts are not normalised (§7), and assert no orthology.
  5. The host species of the isolates is unrecorded, so both liverworts are treated symmetrically; knowing it would let them be weighted.
  6. ESMFold’s low-pLDDT models are ambiguous. Low confidence may mean prediction failure or genuine disorder — small, cysteine-rich, secreted and unstructured-until-bound is a real effector phenotype. The pTM values near 0.1 suggest both are present. The AF3 re-run is what separates them.

12. Notes for anyone re-running this

  • ESMFold must run on an a100. The esm/2.0.0 module ships torch 1.13.1+cu117, built for sm_37–sm_86. On an ada6000 (sm_89), h100 (sm_90) or blackwell6000 (sm_120) it does not refuse — it warns and returns garbage. One run produced 487 nonsense errors and 72 “successful” models that were equally worthless. bin/esmfold_batch.py now checks the device capability against torch.cuda.get_arch_list() and exits.
  • exfab cannot run ESMFold or AF3 — its node has only ada6000.
  • The signalp, esm, predgpi, targetp and dbcanlight modules all activate conda environments via the shell function, so eval "$(conda shell.bash hook)" must precede them.
  • Reusable intermediates do not belong in $SCRATCH. The DuckDB export, candidate FASTA and MMseqs families were written there once and lost when the job ended; they live in analysis/mucor_secretome/cache/ (gitignored) now and rebuild in under three minutes.
  • Slurm’s START_TIME for pending jobs here is a pessimistic placeholder, not a forecast.
