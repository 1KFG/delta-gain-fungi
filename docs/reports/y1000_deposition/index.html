The enriched-Pfam signal is mostly Y1000+ deposition

A companion test to the delta-gain funnel’s family-level result

Author

Stajich Lab

Published

November 16, 2026

Modified

November 22, 2026

What this is

A single targeted test, not a new analysis of the funnel. It asks one question of the funnel’s largest family-level effect — the enriched Pfam families’ +14.6-point excess over an order-matched null — and answers it by joining the existing cluster tables against NCBI BioProject metadata.

It is a companion to the funnel findings, which reports that effect in section 5.1. Read that page’s qualification box first if you have not.

The source of truth is analysis/delta_gain_funnel/ in this repository — code, tests, SLURM driver and the per-family output tables that the numbers below are computed from. This page renders REPORT.md from that directory, so the two cannot drift.

The report is explicit that it does not recompute the +14.6-point figure: doing that needs the order-matched null re-run with the Y1000+ genomes moved into the baseline, which has not been done. It shows the figure rests on a confound, which is a different claim.

The question

The delta-gain funnel’s largest family-level result is that Pfam families chosen for being enriched in new genomes carry +14.6 to +14.9 points more novel clusters than an order-matched baseline null, against +2.2 for size-matched random Pfam controls. The funnel’s own notes observe that the enriched list “is a Saccharomycotina yeast list” and read this as a lineage effect, supported by the fact that it replicates when the list is selected on one half of the species and evaluated on the other.

The objection the split-sample test does not answer: replication across species does not distinguish new biology from a shared deposition history. “Baseline” in the funnel means “had a prior NCBI annotation”. That is a boundary around a database, not around knowledge. A sequencing project that deposits assemblies to GenBank unannotated and releases its own proteomes elsewhere puts every one of its genomes on the new side, however long its proteins have been public — and it does so for all of its species at once, so a split-sample test replicates it perfectly.

The join

Y1000+ is such a project, and it is in BFD at scale.

BioProject NCBI title genomes in BFD
PRJNA736342 Y1000+ Saccharomycotina yeasts genome sequencing and assembly (UW-Madison) 914, across 883 species
PRJNA429441 Sequencing of 196 yeast species from the subphylum Saccharomycotina (UW-Madison) 116

1,033 genomes in total, every one of them on the funnel’s new side. Opulente et al. (2024) state it plainly in their data-availability section: assemblies and raw reads to GenBank, everything else to Figshare. GenBank therefore holds these assemblies without an NCBI annotation, BFD re-annotated them, and their proteins are absent from nr — while the project’s own annotations have been public since 2024 and InterProScan was run across all 1,154 of them.

The test re-asks the funnel’s own question with those genomes moved across the line. A cluster is novel when no member comes from a baseline genome; the second column asks how many clusters have no member from a baseline or Y1000+ genome. Same rule, larger known set.

Result

Enriched Pfam (204) 108,774 77,206 (71.0 %) 19,010 (17.5 %) 75.4 %
Random-control Pfam (197) 51,479 23,796 (46.2 %) 20,879 (40.6 %) 12.3 %

The control is what makes this an argument. Y1000+ contributes ~883 distinct species at roughly one genome each, so it will add singleton clusters to any family; if that were the whole story the control families would shrink too. They lose 12.3 % against the enriched list’s 75.4 %, and their median family loses 0.8 %.

The excess does not merely shrink — it inverts. Before the join the enriched families are 71.0 % novel against the control’s 46.2 %, a 24.8-point excess. After it they are 17.5 % against 40.6 %: 23.1 points below the controls. Whatever “enriched in new genomes” selected for, with Y1000+ counted as public it is the opposite of a novelty-rich set. 1,033 genomes, 5.8 % of the 17,827 new-side genomes in these families, carry three quarters of their novelty.

What this does and does not establish

  • It is not a recomputation of the +14.6-point figure. That statistic is a comparison against an order-matched baseline null with its own resampling; this is the raw novel-cluster share under two definitions of “known”. The direction and size are not in doubt, but the published number should be recomputed properly by re-running the order-matched null with Y1000+ in the baseline, not adjusted by hand from this table.
  • It covers 401 Pfam families, not the corpus. The funnel’s genome-wide figures (50.2 / 29.2 / 22.4 % novel at 95/70/50 %) are untouched by this run. Y1000+ is 5.8 % of the new-side genomes here, and its species are nearly all distinct, so some effect on the genome-wide numbers is likely — it has not been measured.
  • It does not show the enriched families are uninteresting. Yeast-specific families that Y1000+ sampled deeply are real biology; the claim it undercuts is that the funnel’s database comparison discovered them.
  • Y1000+ is the largest such project, not the only one. Any project depositing assemblies without annotation has the same effect. The same join against other large unannotated submissions would say whether this is one project or a general pattern, and that has not been run.

Consequence for the funnel

The funnel’s limitations already state that baseline is “a sampling, not a knowledge boundary” and that the enriched list “is selected on the outcome; it is a candidate list, not a genome-wide estimate.” Both are correct and both are now quantified: for this result the sampling boundary accounts for three quarters of it.

The honest summary of the enriched-Pfam result is that it measures where Saccharomycotina genomes were deposited, and that the lineage reading is not supported once deposition is controlled for. It should not be published as a novelty finding in its present form.

Upstream issue found

SUBPHYLUM is NULL for all 23,680 rows of both samples.parquet and species.parquet in Fungi_BFD_runs/tables/. CLASS (23,462 populated) and ORDER (23,380) are fine, so subphylum-level grouping has to be reconstructed from the class list — this analysis used the nine Saccharomycotina classes present in BFD rather than the subphylum field. Worth fixing where the table is built; it is the second such column found after is_gpi_anchored, which is TRUE for every protein.
